EU Importer of Record 2026: EORI, Direct vs Indirect Customs Representation & Fiscal Representation for China DDP Shipments

A DDP quote puts import clearance on the seller, but EU customs law still needs an identifiable importer inside the customs territory. Duty-paid channels often hide that step until a marketplace asks for an MRN or an audit arrives. This guide separates the legal actors, explains what an EORI does and does not grant, and treats import VAT as a distinct file.

Published 27 November 2026 · Reading time ~8 min · By MJDT Global Logistics Team

Why the Importer of Record Is the First Question in a DDP Quote

Price and transit time are negotiable; the import structure is not. Whoever appears as importer determines who may lodge the declaration, who owes the duty, and whom the authority can pursue years after release. Sellers often assume an all-in duty-paid rate transfers the obligation to the forwarder. It does not: a commercial allocation of cost sits on a different layer from the statutory definition of declarant and debtor. Customs reads the declaration, the identifier and the stated representation type, not your service agreement.

What DDP Actually Commits the Seller To

Under Incoterms 2020, DDP puts the heaviest obligation on the seller: delivery, import clearance, duties and taxes. But Incoterms are contract terms. They allocate expense and risk between the parties; they do not rewrite customs law or create an EU customs identity for a company established only in China. Without an EU entity, an EU party must file on the seller's behalf, and the legal form of that "on behalf of" starts every downstream difference.

Four Roles That Are Constantly Conflated

Keep these separate and most "I thought the forwarder handled everything" disputes disappear.

  • Importer of record: the party accountable to customs for the import and its compliance.
  • Declarant: the person signing the declaration. For release for free circulation, the declarant is the customs debtor.
  • Customs representative: the broker or forwarder appointed to carry out the formalities.
  • Debtor: anyone liable for the duty or import VAT. There can be more than one, jointly.

Goods may belong to A, be declared by B, and leave B and D jointly liable.

EORI Is an Identifier, Not a Permission to Import

The European Commission defines the EORI number as mandatory for all customs operations in the EU customs territory. One person may hold only one valid EORI; it works across the EU and has no automatic expiry. Non-EU operators also need one when lodging declarations, ENS, EXS, temporary storage declarations, or acting as a carrier. An EORI is a customs identifier, not a VAT number, and grants no right to act as importer. Using a broker's EORI where your own company should appear leaves documents and legal subject pointing at different entities.

Direct vs Indirect Representation Under UCC Article 18

Article 18 of the Union Customs Code (Regulation (EU) No 952/2013) lets any person appoint a customs representative. Representation is direct when it acts "in the name of and on behalf of another person" and indirect when it acts "in his own name but on behalf of another person". The representative must in principle be established in the EU customs territory. Article 19 requires the type to be stated at filing; a representative who does not say so, or acts without valid authority, is treated as acting in its own name and on its own behalf.

The Liability Consequence Most Quotes Omit

Article 77(3) makes the declarant the debtor for release for free circulation and adds that, under indirect representation, the represented person is also a debtor. Article 84 makes multiple debtors jointly and severally liable, so either may be pursued for the full amount. Under direct representation the principal is the declarant. Two qualifications matter. In case C-714/20 (12 May 2022) the Court of Justice held that joint liability covers customs duties but does not automatically extend to import VAT unless national law clearly provides for it; member states differ. Article 79 can also reach a person who knowingly supplied false information.

Why Non-EU Sellers Almost Always Land on Indirect Representation

Direct representation presupposes an EU-established principal able to be the declarant itself. A Chinese company without one generally cannot use it, so the workable path is an EU broker filing as indirect representative in its own name, with the seller as represented party and joint duty debtor. That is the real structure behind most duty-paid channels, rarely spelled out on the rate sheet. Ask for the representation type in writing.

Import VAT Is a Separate File Altogether

Customs duty and import VAT are separate debts. Indirect customs representation answers "can this shipment be declared", not "who is registered for VAT, files the return and deducts the input tax". Non-resident businesses frequently cannot register directly and must appoint a licensed fiscal representative to register, file and deal with the tax authority. One DDP programme can therefore hold three parties: an indirect customs representative, a fiscal representative and the seller. Separate their scopes, fees and record-keeping duties in the contract.

The Dutch Article 23 Route, Without the Hype

Under the Article 23 licence of the Dutch Wet OB 1968, import VAT is not collected at the border; it is shifted to the periodic VAT return, declared and, where deductible, deducted on the same return. A foreign company cannot apply itself: the tax authority engages a representative, and conditions include residence, establishment or a fiscal representative in the Netherlands, regular imports, and records showing the import VAT clearly. Representatives come in general and limited forms. A general representative obtains the client's own VAT number and licence and supports intra-EU purchases and B2C sales; a limited one uses the representative's number and covers imports and the first supply only. Set-up runs in weeks, not days, and holding stock in a Dutch warehouse does not by itself create a permanent establishment. Confirm the applicable rate and conditions before quoting.

IOSS Intermediaries for Low-Value Parcels

For B2C consignments of EUR 150 or less, the Import One-Stop Shop is a different route. A non-EU seller normally registers through an intermediary established in the EU, unless based in a third country with a mutual-assistance agreement. With the IOSS VAT number on the import declaration, that import is VAT-free and sales VAT goes into a monthly return, filed even in a month with no sales. IOSS addresses VAT, not customs duty, and does not replace the EORI and representation structure for a bulk DDP import into an EU warehouse. Confirm all thresholds and eligibility with the intermediary in writing.

The Marketplace Layer: MRN and EORI Matching

Marketplaces pushed this structure into the open in 2026. Under platform rules reported by several logistics providers, non-EU sellers sending goods to EU fulfilment centres must supply an EORI and a Movement Reference Number within a set period after receipt, or lose the ability to create new shipments. The MRN is an eighteen-character, one-per-declaration reference from the first import declaration into the EU, not a local clearance number. The point is subject matching: the company behind the EORI must be the actual importer, and a borrowed identifier is treated as invalid. Platform rules change frequently; work from the latest seller notice.

Where the 2026 Customs Reform Is Heading

The new EU customs framework adopted in 2026 makes the direction explicit: for distance sales, importer status is being placed by law on the non-EU seller or its platform, which must be established in the EU or appoint an EU representative holding AEO or trusted-trader status. Public sources still differ on exact application dates, and implementing acts continue to land, so no date is pinned here. Before signing a long-term DDP contract, ask the broker to confirm in writing which rules will apply at the time of import. The space for "the buyer or forwarder quietly absorbs clearance" is narrowing, and subject qualification is becoming an entry condition.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

  • Reading "duty paid" as a transfer of liability. It allocates cost, not statutory debtor status.
  • Borrowing a broker's EORI. The identifier must belong to the importer on the declaration.
  • Leaving representation type off the authority. An unstated type is treated as acting for oneself.
  • Starting a fiscal representative days before arrival. Registrations and licences take weeks.
