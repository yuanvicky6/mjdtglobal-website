China to Israel Food & Cosmetics Imports 2026: MoH Import Licensing, Product Registration & Port Release

A freight quotation answers cost and transit time, not whether a sauce, a sheet mask or a vitamin tablet may be sold in Israel. That answer belongs to the Ministry of Health and must exist before booking. This guide maps the three gates in order.

Published 28 November 2026 · Reading time ~8 min · By MJDT Global Logistics Team

Why a Freight Quote Is Not an Import Approval

Customs clears a consignment against a declaration; the health regulator admits an importer, a product and a warehouse against a standing file. Neither substitutes for the other. Sellers who read a duty-paid rate as product approval discover the gap at the port, when storage starts and a release certificate is missing. Much of the China–Israel e-commerce mix is regulated: food, supplements, cosmetics. Treat regulatory status as a booking precondition, not a repair.

The Three Gates Behind a Regulated Shipment

Israel's official food import guidance describes stages, not one permit. First, a registration certificate from the National Food Services. Second, either confirmation that a regular-food declaration was filed, or an early approval for sensitive food. Third, examination on arrival and a release approval for that shipment, sometimes at the importer's storage site. Cosmetics run a comparable notification sequence. Each stage has its own documents; a failure at any one stops the goods. Approvals take weeks to months; a sailing is booked in days.

Gate 1: The Importer Registration Certificate and Its Warehouse

Only a company, corporation or licensed dealer registered in Israel may apply for an importer registration certificate, which the government calls a necessary and preliminary condition for importing food. It names the importer and the warehouse, belongs to one importer, and cannot be transferred. It runs for two years and may be extended without limit. In a long-term contract that matters: the certificate belongs to the local entity, not the Chinese exporter, and a change of partner means a fresh filing.

The Warehouse Condition Hidden Inside Gate 1

The certificate is tied to a storage site, not just a company name. Threshold conditions require a suitable food storage business licence, or a contract with a licensed storage provider. The importer must hold a valid warehouse certificate from the district health bureau; if the listed warehouses lapse, the registration certificate lapses with them. A supplier switching warehouse mid-programme can invalidate the very document the import depends on. Food of animal origin follows a separate veterinary track with its own permit and approved cold-store agreement.

Gate 2, Track A: Regular vs Sensitive Food

Non-animal-based food is split into two classifications with separate clearance processes, set by regulation rather than the shipper's description. Misclassification delays release: a product declared ordinary can be re-assessed as sensitive and held until the heavier approval exists.

Early Import Approval and the Processing Clock

For sensitive food, the early approval is decided under the public health protection law. Official guidance states a decision within forty-five business days of filing, with shorter indicative targets for complete raw-material or finished-product files and longer ones for infant, task-specific food and supplements. The clock restarts when additional material is supplied, so an incomplete first submission costs far more than the missing page suggests. These are administrative targets, not transit times; confirm the current procedure and fees in writing before fixing a launch date.

The File Format Rules That Reject Applications

A surprising share of rejections is clerical. The official sensitive-food procedure requires separate, clearly scanned PDF files, each named for its content and within a size cap. Documents must be in English; anything else needs a notarised or consular translation into Hebrew or English. Manufacturer documents must be on original letterhead with the factory logo, full address and signing date, and a signature more than one year old is not accepted. Fees are paid only after an inquiry number is issued, and processing starts once payment is registered. Build the file to that specification before packing.

Gate 2, Track B: Cosmetics Move from Licence to Notification

Cosmetics sit under the Pharmacists Ordinance and the Pharmacists (Cosmetics) Regulations, and the direction of travel is from product-by-product licensing towards marketing notification. Public guidance describes the older licensing service as wound down, with ordinary cosmetics on a notification route through a cosmetics portal. A notification is not an endorsement of safety or efficacy; it is a filing that puts the product on the register and assigns responsibility. Confirm the live route in writing before contracting, since transition dates are reported differently. What does not change: a local qualified importer and qualified representative must be appointed; a Chinese brand owner does not file in its own name.

The European Route, the Free Sale Certificate and the Customs Link

The official European Route applies where a qualified representative is appointed, the product is legally marketed in a reference country, it is not a designated cosmetic, the importer holds a quality and safety control plan, and a Free Sale Certificate from a reference-country authority (or a stated alternative) is available. The importer selects the item and route, the representative approves, and the notification is submitted. Here the layers join: the official service states the automatic confirmation serves as an import approval under Section 62(c)(2) of the Customs Ordinance. A correctly filed notification produces the customs-facing permission too.

GMP, the Product Information File and the Local Responsible Person

Israeli law prohibits marketing cosmetics not made under compliant quality control, so the importer or brand owner must hold a declaration of good manufacturing practices. A general ISO 9001 certificate is not sufficient; the referenced standard is ISO 22716, and where no external certificate exists a signed manufacturer declaration or a qualified audit may be used. The declaration is a mandatory part of the Product Information File alongside the safety assessment and formula. Ingredient compliance follows a list-based model close to the EU approach, with prohibited and restricted substances and positive lists checked by INCI name and concentration. One declaration often covers a family from a single site.

Supplements Sit on the Food Side

Dietary supplements are generally handled as a food category, which is why they sit on the sensitive-food side. The file typically includes the quantitative formula, process, specifications, stability data, a safety assessment, the Free Sale Certificate and label artwork, with certified translations and accredited laboratory testing. A local authorised representative is a practical requirement, responsible for official correspondence and post-market obligations. A material change to formula, process or label can require a fresh submission. Confirm the current list with that representative rather than reusing a template from another market.

Gate 3: Port Release Is Per Shipment

Even with an importer certificate and product approval, each consignment needs its own release. Official guidance lists examination on arrival and a release approval for that shipment, with possible examination at the importer's storage site. Temporary measures add a layer: in 2026 the National Food Service issued a time-limited targeted sampling directive covering categories such as dried fruit, nuts and sesame paste, under which release certificates were issued only after satisfactory laboratory results, while products outside it continued under ordinary document review. Verify what is in force at arrival and budget a range for testing.

Where DDP Quietly Fails on Regulated Goods

Incoterms allocate cost and risk; they do not create a regulatory identity. Under DDP the seller takes import clearance and duties, but the importer registration certificate, product approval and qualified representative must still belong to an eligible Israeli entity. Three failures recur: a forwarder's licence assumed to cover a different classification; a partner's certificate expiring or its warehouse changing unnoticed; or goods sailing before the product approval lands, so cargo accrues storage while the file is under review. None is a freight problem or fixed by a faster sailing. Assign each approval to a named holder, with expiry dates and a change-notification clause, before the first order.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid in 2026

  • Treating a DDP quote as product approval. It allocates cost, not regulatory standing.
  • Borrowing a partner's certificate for another category. Classification changes the approval track.
  • Shipping before the approval reference exists. Storage runs while the file is reviewed.
  • Letting a warehouse or formula change go unreported. Either can invalidate the standing approval.
  • Assuming last season's rules still apply. Time-limited measures change; verify at arrival.
