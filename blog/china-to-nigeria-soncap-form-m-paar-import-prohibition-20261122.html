China to Nigeria Shipping 2026: SONCAP, Form M, PAAR & the Revised Import Prohibition List

Nigeria decides an import's fate before the vessel sails. A regulated product needs a Product Certificate, a Form M, a shipment-level SONCAP Certificate and a Customs pre-arrival assessment — all generated at origin, tied to one consistent set of product data. In 2026 the sequence moved onto a single government window, and a revised prohibition list changed which goods may enter at all. This guide sets out the order and where China-side shippers go wrong.

Published 22 November 2026 · Reading time ~8 min · By MJDT Global Logistics Team

Why Nigeria Is a Documentation Lane First

On most lanes the invoice and bill of lading dominate. Nigeria inverts that: much manufactured cargo faces conformity assessment in the country of supply before shipment, and those certificates unlock both the import application and the Customs assessment. A China exporter cannot simply hand over a container and let the buyer clear it, because the buyer cannot clear it without correctly generated origin documents. Missing paperwork is a leading cause of boxes waiting at Lagos with demurrage accruing, so compliance belongs inside the production calendar.

The 2026 Shift: One Window, One Sequence

From 27 March 2026, SON requires Product Certificates, SONCAP Certificates and Form-M-linked import permits to be processed on the National Single Window (NSW). Certificate generation still happens on the SON portal via an accredited inspection firm, but activation — what makes it usable for Form M, PAAR and clearance — now occurs on the NSW.

Separately, the Nigeria Customs Service has been moving Form M, PAAR and duty assessment onto B'Odogwu, its unified system replacing NICIS II through phased rollout since 2024–2025. The two work alongside each other; confirm the exact portal per step with the importer's bank.

  • No long grace period: SON applied the requirement to new applications from the March 2026 date.
  • Origin timing is stricter: SON guidance says SONCAP cannot be processed once goods leave the country of origin; late cases fall under a "SONCAP Default" procedure.

First Question: Is the Product Prohibited?

Screen the HS code against the current prohibition list before ordering any certificate. Under the 2026 Fiscal Policy Measures, the Federal Ministry of Finance issued a revised Import Prohibition List (Trade) by circular dated 1 April 2026, superseding the 2023 measures. It contains 17 categories and applies to goods originating from non-ECOWAS states — where China-origin cargo sits. A 90-day grace covered importers with a Form M and irrevocable trade agreement already opened before that date.

Then: Is It SONCAP-Regulated?

SONCAP is pre-shipment verification that goods meet the applicable Nigerian Industrial Standards or approved equivalents. SON defines scope by exclusion: food, medicines, certain medical items, raw-material chemicals for bona fide manufacturers, military goods, contraband and used products other than automobiles. Electronics, appliances, toys, building materials, machinery and auto parts should be assumed in scope until SON confirms the specific HS code.

Excluded does not mean unregulated. Food, drugs and cosmetics fall under NAFDAC registration, and certain batteries and electrical goods carry producer-responsibility duties under NESREA rules. A failed assessment produces a Non-Conformity Report and the goods should not ship.

The Document Chain, in the Right Order

The common error is reversing the certificates. SON is explicit: the Product Certificate opens Form M, while the SONCAP Certificate processes the PAAR. A pro forma invoice alone does not open Form M for a regulated product.

Form M is a pre-import and foreign-exchange control step, the declaration is the cargo document filed with Customs, and the PAAR sits between them as Customs' risk assessment.

The Product Certificate: PC1, PC2 and PC3

The PC is a longer-lived approval of the product itself, issued after document review, testing and, where required, factory audit. It belongs to the exporter or manufacturer and is what the importer needs before Form M opens.

For a steady lane, moving from PC1 to a registered or licensed route removes repeated testing. The PC records model, brand, HS code and manufacturer, which must match the invoice, SC and PAAR exactly. Confirm current fees with the appointed firm rather than an old forwarding quote.

Form M: What the Importer Must File

Form M is the importer's responsibility, lodged electronically through an authorised dealer bank before shipment and mandatory regardless of value. It needs a registered tax identification number and is built from a pro forma invoice plus an insurance certificate and, for regulated goods, the PC. The bank validates it, Customs registers it, and the resulting number ties the whole file together.

Three disciplines follow: do not ship before registration, because the later certificate, PAAR and declaration all reference it; the invoice, packing list and bill of lading must match its description, HS code, quantity and value, since mismatches routinely trigger queries; and the form has a finite validity, so a long production delay can leave a correct file stale.

The SONCAP Certificate and Pre-Shipment Inspection

Once production is complete, the exporter applies for the shipment-level SONCAP Certificate (Certificate of Conformity). It is per-consignment: one SC serves one PAAR consignment and one entity and cannot be reused. The submission uses the valid PC, registered Form M, final commercial invoice, packing list and transport document, plus the importer's tax details and a full HS classification. Higher-risk categories may attract inspection or loading supervision.

Timing is the constraint. Assessment happens in the country of supply and SON says it cannot start after departure, so the SC must exist before loading, not as the vessel nears Lagos. Importers then activate it on the NSW. Official processing targets exclude testing, corrective work, inspection scheduling and activation — hence the compliance buffer in the production plan.

PAAR and the Inspection Channels

After the validated Form M and final documents are submitted, the Nigeria Customs Service generates a Pre-Arrival Assessment Report. It records HS classification, assessed value and duty, and assigns a channel: green (release after payment), blue (post-release checks), yellow (document check) or red (scanning or physical examination). It replaced the older Risk Assessment Report and is produced before arrival.

The PAAR is where inconsistency gets expensive. SON lists rejected activations for an HS code or item count differing from the PC, a declared value above the PC value, and missing Form M, invoice or bill of lading. A certificate for a different shipment or a carton count mismatch can each hold the file. Treat published service targets as targets, not a promise.

Why Data Consistency Decides Clearance

Almost every "Customs problem" here starts earlier, when importer, supplier, inspection firm and forwarder use different versions of the same data. Form M imposes the first structured version; everything after must agree with it. Freeze one data sheet early — names, TIN, descriptions, HS codes, quantities, values, currency, Incoterm, model and brand — because the HS code drives both duty and which regulator applies, and a value above the PC can block PAAR activation.

Duty, VAT and the Cost Stack

Duty is assessed on a CIF-style import value, with VAT and statutory levies layered on top. The 2026 tax framework sets VAT at a statutory rate, and published Customs build-ups describe a surcharge on duty, a comprehensive import supervision charge and an ECOWAS trade liberalisation levy. These rates are fiscal-policy instruments that move with the annual measures, and the HS heading sets the duty line.

We therefore publish no landed-cost figure here. Build one from the HS code, the confirmed Form M value, the current tariff and levy schedule and the Incoterm, validated by the importer's agent before a DDP price is committed. Understating value creates penalty exposure; overstating it inflates the duty and VAT base. A DDP quote and the compliance file must be built together.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

  • Ordering the SC before a PC exists. The PC opens Form M; the SC serves the PAAR.
  • Shipping before Form M is registered. Every later document references it.
  • Assuming a certificate cures a prohibited item. Screen the HS code first.
  • Letting the invoice drift from Form M on description, value or HS code.
  • Requesting SONCAP after departure, or quoting DDP from an old cost model.
