China to Bangladesh Sea Freight 2026: LCL & FCL, Chittagong Port, Customs & Door-to-Door Delivery

Bangladesh runs on the sea. Almost every bolt of fabric, every yard of cotton yarn, every spare part for the garment industry arrives at Chittagong (or increasingly Mongla) on a vessel from Shenzhen, Ningbo or Shanghai. For cross-border sellers, sea freight is the only mode that scales economically above a few hundred kilograms — but the Chittagong bottleneck, Bangladesh customs paperwork and the Dhaka inland leg each add their own risk. This guide walks through how China-to-Bangladesh sea freight actually works in 2026: LCL vs FCL, realistic transit times, what to file at customs, and how to keep your cargo out of the demurrage yard.

Published 14 September 2026 · Reading time ~9 min · By MJDT Global Logistics Team

Why Ship by Sea to Bangladesh?

Air freight is faster. Sea freight is cheaper — by a wide margin — and for Bangladesh that gap matters more than usual. The country imports raw materials, intermediates and finished goods in large volumes, mostly to feed its garment (RMG) export industry. The cost-per-kg ratio between air and sea on this lane is wide enough that sea is the default for anything above a couple of parcels, and air is reserved for samples, urgent restocks, and high-value electronics.

The trade-off is time and complexity. Where a Shenzhen–Dhaka air shipment clears customs in 24 hours, a sea shipment spends two to five weeks in transit, plus another one to two weeks on import clearance, port handling and the Dhaka inland leg. The right question is therefore not "air or sea?" but "what is the right level of inventory financing versus freight savings for my product?" For most B2B and heavy-goods flows, sea wins on unit cost. For most B2C and time-sensitive flows, air wins — see our China to Bangladesh Air Freight Guide for the other side of that equation.

Chittagong vs Mongla: Pick the Right Port

Bangladesh has two commercial seaports, and the choice between them drives everything downstream:

PortLocationVolume shareBest for
Chittagong (CGP)Southeast coast, near Dhaka~90% of container trafficMost general cargo, garments, electronics, machinery
Mongla (MGL)Southwest, near KhulnaSmaller, growing shareSouthwest Bangladesh, jute, agro goods, less congested routing

Most Chinese-origin cargo enters through Chittagong, because the inland trucking/rail leg to Dhaka is shorter and the deconsolidation and customs facilities are more developed. Mongla is worth considering when goods are destined for the southwest, when Chittagong is congested, or when a particular shipping line offers a faster rotation to Mongla.

Chittagong itself is congested — yard space is tight and vessel berthing windows can slip. Add a buffer in your delivery commitments. A reliable forwarder will track the vessel rotation and pre-book the berth slot before the container reaches port.

LCL vs FCL: When Each Makes Sense

The first decision after picking the port is whether to ship less-than-container-load (LCL) or full-container-load (FCL). The breakeven is roughly 15 CBM of general cargo — below that, LCL is competitive; above it, FCL wins on unit cost and on handling risk.

ModeMin volumeBest forTrade-off
LCL1 CBM+Small first orders, samples, slow-moving SKUs, replenishmentHigher per-CBM rate, more handling, longer deconsolidation
FCL — 20ft~28 CBM payloadSingle-SKU restocks, palletised goods, garments on hangersLocked-in rate per container regardless of fill
FCL — 40ft / 40ft HC~58–68 CBM payloadBulk restock, furniture, appliances, building materialsBest unit cost; requires a loading plan to optimise fill

A common mistake is shipping LCL up to 25 CBM and paying 2–3× more per CBM than an FCL would have cost. Run the math at the breakeven, not just at the headline rate. The breakeven shifts with origin, destination, season and the carrier, so confirm it with a quote rather than a rule of thumb.

Realistic Transit Times in 2026

Door-to-door means from your supplier's loading dock in China to your warehouse or buyer's address in Bangladesh. Realistic end-to-end windows in 2026:

  • Sea LCL, port-to-port: 14–25 days depending on origin and routing (Shenzhen / Ningbo → Singapore / Colombo → Chittagong).
  • Sea FCL, port-to-port: 18–28 days for direct sailings; longer if transshipped via Singapore or Colombo.
  • Sea LCL + customs + inland: 25–40 days door to door (Dhaka inland leg adds 3–7 days).
  • Sea FCL + customs + inland: 28–45 days door to door.

The Dhaka inland leg is where most unexpected delay happens. Chittagong yard congestion, monsoon road closures and a shortage of chassis and trailers all push truck turnaround times up. Forwarders with pre-booked chassis and dedicated trucking partners will reliably deliver in less time than a spot booking.

Bangladesh Sea Customs: What to Prepare

Bangladesh customs (under the National Board of Revenue, NBR) uses the ASYCUDA World electronic system and is document-heavy. Missing paperwork is the single biggest cause of port demurrage charges. The standard import set is:

  1. Commercial Invoice — with HS code, unit price, currency, country of origin, and Incoterm.
  2. Packing List — gross / net weight, dimensions, carton count, marks & numbers.
  3. Bill of Lading — endorsed by the consignee; telex-release accepted by most lines.
  4. Import Permit / License — required for restricted goods (food, medicine, telecom, chemicals).
  5. BSTI clearance — for electronics, food, toys and similar categories, the Bangladesh Standards and Testing Institution requires pre-shipment testing or a conformity certificate.
  6. Insurance Certificate — required for CIF shipments, recommended for all.

Duty and tax composition varies by HS code. A typical consumer-goods import attracts customs duty (CD), a 15% VAT, a supplementary duty (SD) where applicable, and an Advance Income Tax (AIT). A licensed C&F agent at the Bangladesh end is essential — they handle the ASYCUDA filing, pay duties on the importer's behalf and arrange container pickup from the port.

Common pitfall: under-declaring the commercial value to save duty. Bangladesh customs runs valuation checks against international price databases and will reassess, after which the importer pays the original duty plus a 2–5× penalty. The cost of an honest declaration is always lower than the cost of getting caught.

Container Loading Tips for the Bangladesh Lane

A few small loading choices reduce damage claims and clearance friction:

  • ISPM 15 wood packaging. Any wood pallet, crate or dunnage must be heat-treated or fumigated and stamped. Chittagong port rejects untreated wood on sight.
  • Carton labelling. Match marks and numbers on every carton to the packing list exactly. Mismatched labels force the customs officer to open cartons for verification.
  • Moisture control. The Chittagong humidity is high, and the inland leg through monsoon months can dampen unprotected cartons. Silica-gel desiccants and pallet shrink-wrap are cheap insurance for electronics, leather, paper and textiles.
  • Container choice. For garments on hangers, a 40ft high-cube garment-on-hanger (GOH) container is standard. For general goods, standard dry-van containers are fine. Reefer (refrigerated) containers are needed for any cold-chain cargo.

The Bilateral Angle: Garments, RMG and Backhaul

Bangladesh is one of the world's largest garment exporters. That creates a bilateral flow: finished fabrics, accessories, zippers, buttons, packaging and machinery move from China into Bangladesh, while finished garments move from Bangladesh back to China, the EU and North America. If you sell into the RMG supply chain — textiles, trims, packaging, dyes, sewing machinery — the same forwarder can handle your import side and offer competitive rates on the export backhaul. Bundling both flows under one customs and trucking partner usually beats booking each leg separately.

Common Pitfalls on the Bangladesh Sea Lane

  • Don't ship untreated wood packaging. ISPM 15 is non-negotiable; an unmarked pallet at Chittagong is a fast path to a re-export order.
  • Don't under-declare value. NBR valuation cross-checks are routine; under-declared cargo is reassessed with a 2–5× penalty.
  • Don't ignore BSTI. Electronics, food, toys and many other categories require a BSTI certificate before the goods arrive. Filing it after the goods land in port means weeks of demurrage.
  • Don't book with no port buffer. Chittagong yard congestion adds days, sometimes weeks, especially in the monsoon season (May–September) and during Eid.
  • Don't skip a local C&F agent. Trying to self-clear at Chittagong without a licensed agent is, in practice, impossible. A reliable partner is not optional.
