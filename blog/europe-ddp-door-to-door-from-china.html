Europe DDP Shipping from China 2026: Door-to-Door, Customs Clearance & Last-Mile Delivery

"欧洲清提派" — literally "Europe clearance, pickup and delivery" — is the service every Chinese e-commerce seller needs but few forwarders actually provide. It means your goods are cleared through European customs, collected from the port or airport, and delivered to your buyer's door, all under one roof and one price. Here's how it works, what it costs, and how to avoid the mistakes that stall cargo at Rotterdam or Frankfurt for weeks.

Published 7 September 2026 · Reading time ~8 min · By MJDT Global Logistics Team

What "欧洲清提派" Actually Means

Most Chinese freight forwarders stop at the port: they get your container to Rotterdam or Hamburg and their job is done. You're left to find a local customs broker, pay import VAT, arrange a truck, and coordinate last-mile delivery — in a language and tax system you don't know.

清提派 (clearance-pickup-delivery) closes that gap. It bundles three steps into one door-to-door service:

  1. 清关 (Customs clearance) — a licensed EU customs broker files the import declaration, pays duty and VAT, and releases the goods.
  2. 提货 (Pickup / deconsolidation) — the shipment is collected from the port/airport and broken down at a local warehouse.
  3. 派送 (Last-mile delivery) — the goods are delivered to the final address (Amazon FBA warehouse, a retail store, or an end customer's home).

For B2C sellers this is effectively DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) — you pay one all-in rate, and the buyer receives the parcel with nothing extra to pay. That's the single biggest conversion advantage in European e-commerce: no surprise duties at the customer's door.

EU Customs Clearance: The Three Acronyms That Decide Your Shipment

European customs is not one system — it's 27 member states sharing a common framework. Three identifiers determine whether your parcel clears in hours or sits in a bonded warehouse:

  • EORI number — the Economic Operators Registration and Identification number. Required for any business importing into the EU. Without it, customs literally cannot process your declaration.
  • IOSS — the Import One Stop Shop, for B2C goods valued under €150. It lets you collect VAT at checkout and remit it in one place, so the parcel sails through without a VAT stop at the border.
  • HS code — the 6-digit harmonized code (sometimes extended to 8–10 digits per country) that determines the duty rate, which ranges from 0% to 12% for most consumer goods.

A fourth item matters if you sell physical products: CE marking. Electronics, toys, machinery and many other categories must carry CE certification and, since 2021, an EU responsible person on file. Ship without it and your goods can be stopped at the border.

VAT and Duty: How Much You'll Actually Pay

Import cost is a function of the product's HS code and the destination country's VAT rate. Expect:

  • Import duty: typically 0–12%, with many consumer electronics and textiles at 0–8%.
  • VAT: 17–27% depending on country (e.g. Germany 19%, France 20%, Spain 21%, Italy 22%, Hungary 27%). VAT is charged on top of the CIF value plus duty.
  • Handling / broker fees: a flat fee per entry, plus any storage if goods are held for inspection.

For a €10,000 shipment with 4% duty into Germany: duty = €400, VAT base = €10,400, VAT = €1,976. Landed cost ≈ €12,376 — about 24% on top of the goods value. A good DDP quote should already include all of this so there are no surprises.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

  • Don't undervalue the invoice. EU customs cross-checks against market databases and applies the database value plus penalties if you under-declare.
  • Don't ship CE-regulated goods without certification. This is the #1 cause of EU border holds for electronics and toys.
  • Don't forget the EU responsible person. Since 2021, products sold into the EU need a designated responsible entity for compliance contact.
  • Don't treat the UK as "Europe" for customs. Post-Brexit, the UK has its own customs border and VAT rules — it's a separate clearance.
