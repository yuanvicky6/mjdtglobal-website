China to Australia Shipping 2026: Air vs Sea Freight, Customs & Door-to-Door Delivery

Australia is one of the most stable and predictable lanes out of China for cross-border sellers — a mature market with a high online-spend habit and a single, unified customs system. But it also has the strictest biosecurity border in the world. Here's how to move cargo from Shenzhen to Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane or Perth without delays, extra charges, or a quarantine hold.

Published 8 September 2026 · Reading time ~8 min · By MJDT Global Logistics Team

Air Freight vs Sea Freight: The Core Trade-Off

The China–Australia lane is short enough by sea that many sellers default to ocean freight for restocking, and reserve air freight for fast-moving or high-value SKUs. The gap between the two is mostly time and cash flow, not reliability — both are mature, scheduled services out of Shenzhen, Guangzhou and Hong Kong.

For light e-commerce parcels, air freight often wins on total landed cost once you factor in inventory financing and the speed of turning stock into sales. For heavier or lower-margin goods, sea freight's lower unit cost usually dominates. Many sellers run a hybrid: air for the fast movers, sea for the base stock.

Door-to-Door Transit Times from China

Door-to-door adds customs clearance and last-mile delivery on top of the line-haul. Realistic end-to-end windows are:

  • Air express / air + courier last-mile: typically 5–10 days door to door.
  • Air freight + customs + local trucking: 6–12 days depending on clearance speed.
  • Sea LCL + last-mile: 25–40 days door to door.
  • Sea FCL + last-mile: 30–45 days door to door.

Sydney and Melbourne are the two largest entry points, with Brisbane, Fremantle (Perth) and Adelaide serving the rest. Your last-mile cost and timing depend heavily on whether the goods land near the final delivery postcode — a Melbourne landing for a Perth customer adds days and cost.

Australian Customs: GST, Duty and the Border Force

Australia runs a single national customs authority — the Australian Border Force (ABF) — which keeps the rules consistent whether your goods enter through Sydney, Melbourne or any other port. The key charges on import are:

  • GST (10%): applied to most imported goods on the customs value plus duty plus freight and insurance. For goods sold to consumers, GST is generally collected at the point of sale under the low-value imported goods rules.
  • Import duty: most consumer goods attract 0–5%, though some categories (textiles, footwear, certain vehicles and alcohol/tobacco) run higher. The exact rate is driven by the HS code.
  • Processing and broker fees: a flat per-entry fee for the customs declaration, plus any inspection or storage if the goods are held.

Quoting DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) removes the buyer-side friction: your forwarder clears the goods, pays GST and duty, and delivers with nothing owed at the door. For Amazon and marketplace sellers this is usually the cleanest option — the customer sees one price and no surprise charges.

Biosecurity: The Step That Catches People Out

Australia's biosecurity regime (run by the Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry) is the strictest single hurdle on this lane. Shipments are screened for plant material, soil, pests, food and animal products, and regulated goods require permits before they even depart. The practical rules that affect most sellers:

  • Wood packaging (pallets, crates, dunnage) must be ISPM 15 compliant — heat-treated or fumigated and stamped. Untreated timber is a fast path to a quarantine hold or re-export at your cost.
  • Clean your goods and containers. Soil, seeds, bark and live insects trigger mandatory treatment or destruction.
  • Certain categories are restricted or prohibited — food, agricultural products, wooden items, cosmetics with certain ingredients, and batteries (which are also a dangerous-goods concern by air).

A forwarder who pre-checks your HS codes against biosecurity requirements before departure saves you weeks and re-inspection fees on arrival. This is the single biggest reason to work with a specialist on the Australia lane rather than a generalist.

For B2C sellers, DDP is the default recommendation: Australian consumers expect all-inclusive pricing. For B2B buyers registered for GST, DAP can be attractive because they reclaim the GST as an input tax credit — a point worth confirming with your buyer before you quote.

Selling into Amazon FBA Australia

Amazon's Australian marketplace (amazon.com.au) continues to grow, and FBA sellers there face the same core logistics decisions as on any other site:

  • Labelling and compliance before the goods leave China — FBA has strict carton and product labelling rules, and fixing labels at an Australian facility is expensive.
  • Delivery to the right FC. Amazon assigns fulfilment centres; your forwarder needs to deliver to the specific warehouse with a booked appointment, not just "somewhere in Sydney."
  • GST registration. Depending on your turnover, you may need to register for GST in Australia and collect it on sales.

Combining China-side prep with an Australia door-to-door DDP service is the cleanest path: goods are cleared, labelled, and delivered to the FC in one chain.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

  • Don't ship untreated wood packaging. ISPM 15 compliance is non-negotiable; a quarantine hold costs more than the pallet did.
  • Don't forget the GST on your landed cost. Under-quoting DDP by leaving GST out of your margin is a common margin killer.
  • Don't mix prohibited items into a consolidated carton. One restricted item can hold up the entire shipment for inspection.
  • Don't under-declare value. The ABF cross-checks declarations and under-valuation attracts penalties.
