China to Bangladesh Letter of Credit 2026: LCAF, L/C Terms & Documentary Discipline

On this lane the shipment starts at a bank, not a factory. Outward foreign exchange is controlled, most imports are financed through an irrevocable credit, and its wording decides which documents must exist before a container moves. This guide covers the LCAF authorization, the identifiers on every page, and why a bank mismatch can cost more than a late vessel.

Published 25 November 2026 · Reading time ~8 min · By MJDT Global Logistics Team

Why the L/C, Not the Vessel, Sets the Clock

Shippers tend to plan backwards from a sailing date. Here the binding date is often earlier: the credit must be authorized, opened and accepted, and the goods produced, inspected and shipped inside its windows. A vessel can be rebooked; an expired credit or a refused presentation cannot. The invoice, packing list and bill of lading are not just customs paperwork, they are what a bank examines before paying. Payment and shipping files must be built together, from the same facts, before loading.

The Import Policy Order and the Authorized Dealer Channel

Bangladesh regulates imports through an Import Policy Order from the commerce ministry and foreign-exchange rules administered by Bangladesh Bank. Outward payment moves through an Authorized Dealer bank, and except in defined cases the instrument is an irrevocable letter of credit. The order sets who may import, prohibited or restricted goods, and category conditions. Successive orders have also created bands where smaller consignments settle on contract and telegraphic transfer rather than a credit; those thresholds have been revised repeatedly and must be confirmed against the order in force at booking. Let the importer's bank confirm the route.

The LCAF: The Form That Gates Every Shipment

Before a credit is opened, the importer's Authorized Dealer issues a Letter of Credit Authorization Form, abbreviated LCAF or LCA. It is the controlling document: importer, goods, value, currency, HS code and validity. Its limits propagate. The credit cannot exceed the LCAF amount, and on FOB terms FOB value plus freight must stay within it, so a growing order needs an amended form. It also carries a finite validity, commonly months up to a year. Its number is not a bank-internal reference: credits routinely require it printed on the invoice, packing list and bill of lading, reconciled with the import registration on file.

IRC, BIN and TIN: Three Numbers That Must Agree

A commercial importer needs an Import Registration Certificate from the Chief Controller of Import and Export, a Tax Identification Number, and a Business Identification Number for customs filings. They are not interchangeable, and banks have been told to verify the IRC is the original, current copy, not an expired or forged one. Credits commonly require the LCAF number, IRC number and HS code on the invoice, packing list and bill of lading, and the BIN on all import documents. A wrong digit creates a discrepancy. Confirm the strings in writing and lock them into a template so each page matches.

Two Document Sets, One Set of Facts

Two files are often confused. The customs file is what the clearing agent uses at Chattogram or Mongla for release: bill of entry, invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin and the inspection report. Our shipping documents and customs clearance guide covers that side. The bank file is the presentation under the credit, and it is stricter: descriptions, quantities, values and marks must match the credit and not conflict across documents. A customs officer may accept a clarification; a document examiner compares text. The answer is one master data set behind both, so a change to the invoice is never left unreflected.

Why CFR, Not CIF, and the Insurance Declaration

To protect the domestic insurance market, CIF imports are generally not permitted without ministry authorization. Credits therefore call for CFR or FOB and add conditions to replace the missing seller-side cover: a prompt shipping advice, a beneficiary declaration, and a statement that the applicant has placed insurance, naming the insurer and policy number. Exporters sometimes misread a related invoice notation as a typo. "Import under CFER" means Cash Foreign Exchange Resources, settlement in convertible foreign exchange; it is not a spelling error. Produce exactly the declarations asked for and confirm the insurance arrangement before sailing.

PSI and the Clean Report of Findings

Bangladesh has long required pre-shipment inspection for most imports, by government-appointed agencies against the proforma invoice, packing list and applicable standards. Scope covers quality, quantity, classification and declared value, and the outcome is a Clean Report of Findings. The critical detail is the endorsement: the agency endorses the invoice and packing list, and the set presented to the bank must be identical to that version. A reissued invoice with cleaner wording is a reliable route to a discrepancy and a customs query. Arrange inspection once production is complete, keep the endorsed originals, and never regenerate documents after endorsement.

UCP 600 and the Five Banking Days

Credits here are usually subject to UCP 600, the ICC rules for documentary credits, and are known for long field 47A conditions. Banks examine documents, not goods. UCP 600 gives the issuing bank a maximum of five banking days following presentation to examine and decide; if it refuses, a single notice must state every discrepancy, and a late or incomplete notice can preclude reliance on it. Article 14 provides that data need not be identical elsewhere provided it does not conflict; Article 18 requires the invoice goods description to correspond with the credit. Extra detail is not automatically a discrepancy, but arguing that after the fact is a poor substitute for clean documents.

Where Discrepancies Actually Come From

Most refusals are mundane: a goods description shortened or expanded relative to the credit; a bill of lading notify party lacking a city; presentation after expiry or beyond the stated period; a missing signature, stamp or beneficiary certificate; an invoice that no longer matches the endorsed PSI copy; a shipment date outside the allowed window. Even a spelling difference can be raised, though not every difference is valid under the rules and standard banking practice. The defence is procedural: read the credit on arrival, list every condition, and request amendments for anything impossible before production. Chasing an amendment after sailing is where costs accumulate.

Bank Risk Is a Separate Question

Documentary discipline manages one risk; the issuing bank's standing is another. Trade-promotion advisories note that some banks here have delayed honouring sight credits without a cited discrepancy, released documents before payment, or been drawn into post-delivery quality disputes, and financial stress has produced consolidations and payment delays. Exporters are advised to check the applicant and issuing bank before agreeing terms, seek a partial advance or a confirmed credit, consider export credit insurance, and consult the risk notices published by China's economic and commercial office in Bangladesh. A 2025 claim that the central bank had withdrawn its backing for credits was a misreading of circulars about non-credit business; the irrevocable framework was not abolished. Verify regulatory news at source.

A Pre-Shipment Discipline Checklist

  • Policy route: confirm with the importer's bank whether a credit is required or a transfer-settlement band applies.
  • Registration: verify a current IRC and lock the exact IRC, TIN, BIN, LCAF and HS strings into one data set.
  • Credit review: read field 47A line by line; list shipment dates, presentation period, certificates and the goods description.
  • Terms: confirm the Incoterm is CFR or FOB, and plan the shipping advice and insurance declaration.
  • Inspection: book PSI after production; keep and freeze the endorsed invoice and packing list.
  • Consistency: reconcile invoice, packing list, bill of lading and markings against the credit.
  • Timing: present inside the window and track the five-banking-day clock with your advising bank.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid in 2026

  • Treating the credit as post-loading paperwork, not a specification to build to.
  • Quoting an old settlement threshold instead of confirming the current order.
  • Letting LCAF value or validity lapse as the order grows, then shipping against it.
  • Reissuing the invoice after the PSI endorsement, or letting the bank set differ from it.
  • Assuming minor wording differences are harmless until a refusal notice arrives.
  • Confusing the customs file with the bank file, and reconciling only one.
