China to Australia FBA Shipping 2026: Amazon AU, GST & Door-to-Door DDP

Australia looks like an easy lane on paper — one national border authority, an English-speaking market, and a short ocean transit. In practice, three things trip up Chinese sellers: the GST low-value imported goods rules, Amazon AU's inbound requirements, and the strictest biosecurity screening in the world. This guide covers how to structure a China-to-Australia supply chain so your cargo arrives at the fulfilment centre, not in a quarantine hold.

Published 16 September 2026 · Reading time ~9 min · By MJDT Global Logistics Team

Why Australia Deserves Its Own Supply-Chain Playbook

Most sellers treat Australia as an extension of their UK or US operation. That usually works until the first GST question, the first FBA appointment rejection, or the first biosecurity inspection fee lands. The lane has its own logic:

  • Single national customs regime. The Australian Border Force (ABF) applies the same rules at every port, so once your HS codes and valuation method are right, they are right everywhere.
  • GST is collected at the point of sale for consumers. Under the low-value imported goods (LVIG) framework, the tax obligation shifts to the seller or the platform rather than the consumer at the door.
  • Biosecurity is the real bottleneck. Wood packaging, plant material, soil and certain consumer categories are screened hard, and holds are expensive in time and money.
  • Amazon AU is small but high-value per capita. Competition is thinner than in the US, which rewards sellers who can restock reliably.

The GST Question: What Actually Applies to Your Shipment

Australia applies a 10% Goods and Services Tax. Where it is charged depends on who you are and what you are shipping — and this is the single most misunderstood part of the lane.

SituationWho accounts for GSTPractical implication
B2C sales to Australian consumers, low-value goodsSeller or platform under LVIG rulesGST is embedded in the retail price; you collect and remit it rather than paying at the border
B2B import for a GST-registered buyerImporter of recordBuyer may claim the GST back as an input tax credit — DAP can be preferable to DDP
Bulk import into your own Australian entityYour entity, at importGST payable on the customs value plus duty plus international freight and insurance
Goods delivered DDP to a consumerSeller / seller's forwarderOne all-in price, nothing owed at the door — the cleanest B2C experience

The takeaway: GST does not disappear because you ship DDP — it moves. If you quote DDP, the GST must sit inside your landed-cost model, or you will quietly sell at a loss on every unit. Confirm the treatment with your own tax adviser, since registration thresholds and platform obligations depend on your structure.

Choosing Your Australian Entry Point

Where your container lands drives both inland trucking cost and how fast it reaches Amazon's fulfilment network. There is no universally "best" port — there is a best port for your delivery postcodes.

Port Botany (Sydney)NSW, ACTAustralia's largest container gateway; strong choice for Australia's biggest consumer population

A common mistake is landing everything in Sydney because it is the biggest port, then paying to truck pallets to Perth. Matching the entry port to the destination state is one of the simplest ways to cut inland freight.

Amazon AU FBA Prep: The China-Side Checklist

Most FBA problems on this lane are created before the goods ever leave China. Fixing them at origin costs a fraction of fixing them in Australia.

  1. Confirm the assigned fulfilment centre before booking freight. Amazon AU allocates FCs and expects a booked delivery appointment. Shipping to "Sydney" is not the same as shipping to a specific dock door.
  2. Apply FBA labels and carton markings in China. Relabelling at an Australian third-party facility adds handling, storage and delay for work that is free at origin.
  3. Check ASIN-level compliance. Specific categories carry Australian standards, labelling or documentation requirements that differ from the US and EU listings you may already have approved.
  4. Verify battery and dangerous-goods status. Lithium batteries require the correct UN classification, packaging and documentation, and by air they are a hard constraint.
  5. Keep carton weights and dimensions within FBA limits. Overweight cartons are refused or reworked, and rework charges are the seller's.

Biosecurity: The Compliance Layer Most Sellers Underestimate

Australia's biosecurity framework is administered separately from customs, and it is genuinely strict. The practical implications for commercial cargo:

  • All wood packaging must be ISPM 15 compliant — heat-treated or fumigated, and visibly stamped. Untreated pallets, crates and dunnage are a direct route to a hold, treatment charge, or re-export.
  • Cleanliness matters. Soil, seeds, bark and live insects on goods or containers trigger mandatory intervention.
  • Some categories need permits before departure. Food, plant-based products, wooden articles, certain cosmetics and animal-derived materials are regulated — permits obtained late can strand goods at origin.
  • Consolidated cartons are a shared risk. One non-compliant item can pull the whole consignment into inspection, delaying compliant sellers in the same container.

Pre-screen HS codes and material composition against biosecurity requirements before booking. It is the highest-leverage compliance step on this lane.

Building a Landed-Cost Model You Can Trust

Australia punishes sellers who compare quotes on freight rate alone. A usable landed-cost model includes:

  • Origin charges — pickup, export customs, terminal handling, documentation.
  • International freight — sea or air, plus any peak-season or GRI surcharges.
  • Destination charges — port/terminal fees, destination handling, delivery order.
  • Duty, calculated on the customs value by HS code.
  • GST, wherever it lands in your structure.
  • Clearance, brokerage, inspection and any quarantine intervention costs.
  • Inland trucking to the FC or final address, including appointment handling.
  • Inventory carrying cost between departure and availability.

That last line is the one most spreadsheets omit. Faster modes cost more per kilogram but release cash sooner; the correct answer depends on your sell-through rate, not on the freight quote alone.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

  • Quoting DDP without GST in the model. This is the most common margin killer on the lane.
  • Landing on the wrong coast. Choosing a port by reputation rather than by destination postcode inflates inland freight.
  • Shipping untreated timber. ISPM 15 is not optional, and holds cost far more than compliant pallets.
  • Booking freight before the FC assignment. Goods with nowhere to be delivered accrue storage and re-handling.
  • Under-declaring value. The ABF cross-checks declarations; penalties and delays far outweigh any duty saved.
