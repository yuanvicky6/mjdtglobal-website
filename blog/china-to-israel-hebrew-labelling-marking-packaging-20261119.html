China to Israel 2026: Hebrew Labelling, Marking & Packaging Compliance for Imports

Customs release is not the same as market access. Israel requires imported goods to carry Hebrew labelling, name a local importer and state origin and contents in metric units before they can be sold. This guide sets out what the label must say, how it must be applied, the category overlays for food and cosmetics, and the 2028 food-labelling reform already changing artwork decisions.

Published 19 November 2026 · Reading time ~8 min · By MJDT Global Logistics Team

Why the Label Is a Second Gate

Most exporters treat Israeli customs as the finish line. In practice there are two gates: customs decides whether a consignment may enter, while the marking rules decide whether the same cartons may be sold once inland. A shipment can clear at Haifa or Ben Gurion and still be stopped by a retailer or a buyer's goods-in check because the outer carton carries no Hebrew text. Once units are shrink-wrapped on a pallet, adding compliant marking becomes manual rework at a bonded facility, with handling and storage attached — a risk DDP sellers carry themselves.

Two Gates: Product Approval vs Marking

It helps to separate this from the standards question. Whether a product needs an SII approval, a type approval or a Ministry of Health registration is an admissibility question — whether it may be sold at all. Marking asks what information must appear on an admissible article, in what language and form. A consignment can hold every approval it needs and still fail because the importer's address is missing from the retail pack. The label is a separate workstream with its own sign-off.

The Legal Anchor: Hebrew Is Mandatory

The rule is anchored in Israel's consumer-protection legislation and its marking regulations, complemented by sector-specific food and standards law. In all instances Hebrew must be used. English, Arabic or Russian may be added, but English characters may not be larger than the Hebrew ones — a detail that trips up artwork built around a Latin brand name. Official guidance advises clearing label text with the Israeli importer before shipping, since that party's name and address must appear. Hebrew covers the mandatory particulars, not just a translated brand story: warnings, ingredient lists and date markings fall within it where compulsory.

Two observations matter for China-origin cargo. The Israeli importer field is not optional — a DDP parcel sent to an end consumer still needs a local responsible entity named on the pack, which is why DDP programmes appoint one up front. And metric units are required: packaging printed for a market using ounces or inches needs a reprint or an over-label, not a conversion note on the invoice.

How the Marking Must Be Applied

The rules reach beyond content into method. Marking should be printed, engraved, stamped or durably applied; where that is impossible, a label must be securely stuck on. Details must be legible, contrast with the background and not damage the goods. On multi-layer packaging the external layer is marked; where a pack contains sub-packages, the label states their number and the net content of each. Aerosols state net quantity by weight for semi-solids and by volume for liquids. A translucent sticker over transparent film, or a label that peels in a humid container, fails on durability rather than content — and is discovered after arrival.

Stick-On Labels: What Is and Is Not Allowed

Israel permits stick-on labels for prepacked consumer products, the mechanism most China-origin programmes rely on: a compliant Hebrew label can be applied to the retail pack before consolidation without a dedicated carton print. The added label must carry the mandatory particulars legibly, be firmly affixed, and must not obscure the batch number, production date or expiry date — a specific concern in cosmetics and food, where traceability depends on them. Where a pack is too small, an outer box or leaflet can carry the overflow, but the core particulars must stay visible at the point of sale. Importers keep an approved template and a translation sign-off per SKU.

Food deserves separate attention because it is midway through a legislative change. Cosmetics is where label artwork and registration interlock most tightly — an approved formulation and a non-compliant label are two different failures, and both must be cleared before shipment.

The 2028 Food Labelling Reform

Israel has adopted the EU food information regulation, Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011, through Amendment No. 10 to the Public Health Protection (Food) Law, 2015. It has applied voluntarily since 1 January 2025 and becomes mandatory for all food business operators on 1 January 2028. Until then the existing regime, including mandatory standard SI 1145 on food labelling, continues to govern, and local specific legislation prevails where both touch the same food. The consolidated text is published on the Ministry of Health portal.

The compulsory declarations include the food name, ingredients, allergens, net content, production and use-by dates, storage and use conditions, manufacturer details, and — for imported food — the importer's name and address and the country of production. A nutrition declaration is compulsory on packaged foods. Hebrew applies to the packaging; other languages are optional. Mandatory particulars use characters with an x-height of at least 1.2 mm. Transitional phasing applies to some animal products; imported fish came within the adopted provisions from 1 August 2026 while raw meat remained outside them. For packaging printed in 2026, design to the incoming regime, because a plate ordered now will still be in use after the mandatory date.

Where Labelling Sits in a DDP Chain

On a DDP shipment the seller owns the clearance and the delivered price, so it also owns the compliance work a receiver would otherwise do. Marking can happen at the factory before consolidation, at a consolidation warehouse in China, or as a value-added service at an overseas warehouse; each trades artwork control against unit handling and rework risk. Origin is cleanest for a repeat SKU, while a pre-shipment over-label suits mixed-SKU or test quantities. What Non-Compliance Actually Costs

Penalties depend on the category, the authority and the point of detection, so the useful view is operational: goods held for re-marking, re-labelling under supervision, refusal of release, return to origin, or destruction where food or cosmetics lose traceability. Each carries handling and storage and lands on whoever contracted to deliver. A retailer rejection after clearance is worse than a border hold. All of it is avoidable if the label is reviewed before production.

A Pre-Shipment Labelling Checklist

  • Confirm the Israeli importer or responsible entity and secure its exact legal name and address for the artwork.
  • Screen the SKU for category overlays — food, cosmetics, electrical, toy, chemical — before designing the label.
  • Build the mandatory particulars in Hebrew, with any English no larger than the Hebrew text.
  • Use metric units and state net, not gross, content; check sub-package and outer-carton rules.
  • For food, design to the 2028 adopted regime and record which standard version the artwork follows.
  • Decide where marking happens and who signs off the applied result; file a photo with the shipment documents.

Common Mistakes to Avoid in 2026

  • Assuming customs release equals market access. Marking is enforced at retail too.
  • Treating SII or Ministry of Health approval as a substitute for compliant labelling.
  • Omitting the Israeli importer's address on direct-to-consumer DDP parcels.
  • Covering the original batch or expiry date with a Hebrew sticker.
  • Leaving over-labelling until after arrival, when rework is slowest and storage is running.
